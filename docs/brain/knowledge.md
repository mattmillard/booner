# Hunting brain — expert core

The analyst (api/src/brain.ts) loads this file and `field-knowledge.md` as its standing knowledge on every run.
Evidence tags: **[R]** peer-reviewed / GPS-collar research, **[E]** expert consensus of proven pressured-deer hunters,
**[P]** physics, **[F]** folklore — show it, weight it ~0. **[H]** = this hunter's own field record (see field-knowledge.md);
[H] outranks [E] for his ground once it repeats.

Sources: docs/research/05-bowhunting-expertise.md (science + model), docs/research/06-pro-bowhunters.md (Eberhart,
Infalt, Litzinger, May, The Hunting Public and similar pressured/public-land hunters), and the hunter's journal.

## 1. What actually moves mature bucks (weights)
- **Rut phase and time of day dominate** [R]. Central MO: pre-rut Oct 23–31, seeking/chasing Nov 1–7, peak breeding
  Nov 8–24 (archery closed Nov 14–24), post-rut, second rut ~Dec 1–15, late season food + cold.
- **Temperature below normal** is the most consistent weather driver [R]; heat (>75–80°F) suppresses daylight movement [R].
- **Cold fronts**: small real effect, mostly captured by temperature drop; the first cold, clear, NW-wind sit after a
  warm spell is where hunters report their best sits [E]. Don't double count with temperature.
- **Barometric pressure**: absolute value ~no effect [R]; 3–12 h *change* weakly predictive and inconsistent [R-w].
- **Rain**: bucks move less in steady rain [R]; first dry hours after rain is hunter lore [E].
- **Moon phase / solunar**: GPS studies show trivial effects [R] → weight 0, but **record it** — if this hunter's own
  data shows a repeatable moon pattern on his ground, say so with the sample size.
- **Hunting pressure is large and measurable** [R]: bucks avoid a hunted stand ~3 days, return by ~day 6; 12 h of sits in a
  week halves daylight use. Mature bucks shift *where and when*, not *away*. First sit on a fresh stand is the best sit.

## 2. Thermals and wind — why the hunter keeps stressing them
Scent control is the whole game for a mature buck; the wind forecast alone lies at ground level in hill country.
- **Night / evening**: cooling air sinks and drains **downhill and down drainages**, pooling in hollows and bottoms [P].
  Evening thermals fall roughly the last 30–60 min of light (Sturgis) — a stand above deer travel in the evening
  pours scent onto the trail; be **level with or below** travel in the evening.
- **Morning**: air keeps draining until the slope is sunlit, then flips uphill. East/south slopes flip first; north
  faces, shaded hollows and creek bottoms flip last (1–3 h after sunrise in MO hollows — an estimate, **learn it from the
  hunter's data**). In the morning be **above** travel.
- **Transitions swirl**. Light synoptic wind (<5 mph at ground) → thermals dominate; >10–12 mph → wind dominates except in
  lee eddies. Overcast weakens thermals.
- **Cold-air pools** (bottoms, hollows): before ~1.5 h after sunrise and after sunset, scent drains down-drainage
  regardless of forecast wind — morning stands in bottoms are dangerous for anything downstream.
- **Deer use it**: mature bucks bed with wind at their back and eyes downhill, on the upper third / leeward side where
  prevailing wind and rising thermals converge (Infalt) [E]; they approach food and beds from downwind and J-hook in [E];
  in the rut they cruise the downwind side of doe bedding [E].
- **Deer-free scent sinks** are gold: lakes, ponds, rivers, bluff drops, open fields in daylight, roads — let the plume
  fall there. (Water is a scent sink, never a place deer walk past a stand — see field-knowledge #L1.)

## 3. Where mature bucks move in daylight (terrain/cover)
Screening cover (2× cover at used beds [R]), upper-third benches/points, leeward ridges, saddles, pinch points between
cover and open, inside corners, creek crossings with low banks, draws/ditches through ag, thermal hubs where draws meet,
islands of cover far from roads. Daylight corridors run through cover between beds and food; fields are night food
(bucks use plots 2–5× more at night) [R] — daylight opportunity is on the corridor and in staging cover, not the field.

## 4. Pressured / public-land hunter rules [E] (research/06: Eberhart, Infalt, Litzinger, May, The Hunting Public)
- **Pressure comes from parking, not roads.** 87% of PA public-land hunters stay within 0.5 km of a road; odds of an
  area being hunted drop ~3× per extra 500 m walked and ~1.5× per extra 5° of slope [R]. 90% of GA WMA pressure sat on
  51% of the land [R]. Yet these hunters kill bucks 60–100 yd from roads that have **no parking** and a cover screen.
  Barriers (water, waders/canoe, steep climbs, thick cover) filter out hunters better than distance does.
- **First sit kills.** Infalt: 11–12 of his top 15 bucks came on the first sit of a spot; Andy May ~80%. Eberhart hunts
  a secure spot only 3–4 times a season. Save "A" stands for the best wind + phase + cold snap; log every sit.
- **Distance to the bed scales with cover:** marsh 75–100 yd, hills/forest 100–200 yd (Infalt); 50–100 yd on a
  pressured nocturnal buck (Kenyon); 30–75 yd downwind after a soft bump (May). An occasional bump means the distance is
  right; bumping every sit means too close.
- **Bed sets:** buck beds with thick cover/wind at his back, watching downwind or open ground; set on his exit trail
  toward the evening destination, crosswind, with the plume off the bed, the trail and his downwind scent-check arc.
  Mornings need entry ≥90 min before light without crossing the feed→bed trail. Evenings only when the evening drainage
  carries scent *away* from bed and trail (down a ravine / into a cold-pool or water sink); Sturgis says mornings only.
- **Rut midday is gold:** Eberhart spent <15% of rut hours on stand 11 AM–4 PM yet killed 35% of his mature rut bucks
  then; Infalt sees cruising 9 AM–2 PM. Pre-rut: primary scrape clusters (≥3 scrapes/licking branch) 10 AM–3 PM,
  roughly peak breeding −14…−5 days. Seeking: 40–100 yd off the downwind edge of doe bedding, bed-to-bed funnels,
  leeward upper-third ridge lines. Public land: the last week of October beats the crowded early-November vacation.
- **Sign:** ignore night sign in the open; project it toward cover near beds. Big rubs mean a mature buck; a rub line
  shows direction; fresh (<24 h) rut sign beats location for 2–5 days. A bumped buck usually re-beds within ~300 yd.
- **Access:** final 100 yd into the wind or up a creek/draw bed; water routes leave no ground scent; wet leaves and
  ≥10 mph wind/rain cover noise; accept up to +30 min walk to avoid deer. Stand height 18 ft default, 25 ft+ on
  pressured public ground. Weather only reorders sites; it never cancels a day.
- **Don't encode:** Eberhart's "ignore wind with carbon clothing" (everyone else disagrees); "deeper is always better".
- **Area rules vary:** e.g., some MDC areas ban trail cameras and allow only portable stands (no screw-in steps,
  removed by Feb 1; blinds removed daily). Check each area's regulations link before planning a set.

## 5. The season in central Missouri (research/07; anchor: peak breeding P = Nov 13 ±3 d, set by day length)
| Phase (2026) | Food that holds does/bucks | Best sits | Stand type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early, Sep 15–Oct 4 | **Green soybeans** (top forage; deer pile in before dusk [H]), alfalfa, first white oak acorns | **Evening**, last 45 min; mornings only with a clean bed-side entry | field edge / staging 30–150 yd inside cover |
| Lull, Oct 5–22 | **White oak acorns** (drop ~Sep 25–Oct 10, eaten out in ~2 wk), harvested fields at dark | Evening near beds, morning on mast; few sits, save A-stands | oak flats, bed↔food transitions 75–200 yd off beds |
| Pre-rut, Oct 23–Nov 5 | Does still on the best food (red oaks, waste grain, green wheat) | **Dawn and dusk prime** [H], mornings ≥ evenings; midday on cold fronts | scrape clusters (peak ~Oct 27–Nov 1), rub lines, downwind of doe bedding |
| Seeking, Nov 6–12 | Food matters only as doe locations | **All day** (9 AM–2 PM strong) | funnels between doe bedding, saddles, inside corners, crossings |
| Peak, Nov 13–20 (archery closed Nov 14–24) | — | all day in doe cover | doe-bedding perimeters/interiors |
| Post-rut Nov 21–Dec 4 / 2nd rut Dec 5–18 | Recovery feeding; doe-fawn estrus ~Dec 2 | **Evening** on food | staging near the best remaining food |
| Late, Dec 19–Jan 15 | Standing beans/corn, waste grain, green wheat, brassicas after frost, red oaks | **Evening**, earlier on cold sunny days | food edge next to thermal cover |
- The October "lull" is a **hunter** lull: buck movement is flat until ~Oct 20, then climbs steeply; deer are in the
  timber on acorns. ~85% of scrape visits are at night; mature bucks rarely show on scrape cameras.
- **Crops move deer more than the calendar Sep–early Nov.** Soybean leaf drop and harvest, and corn harvest, happen
  Sep 20–Nov 10. Each harvested field deletes food (and for corn, cover) overnight: move stands with it. Corn harvest
  displaces deer into nearby permanent cover (draws, bottom timber, CRP) for ~3 weeks. The app follows the NASS
  Missouri progress curves (`web/src/seasonal.ts`), shifted for the current year and calibrated to the hunter's fields.
- Mast years matter: a bumper white-oak crop pulls deer off fields (lower field sightings); a failure pushes them onto
  ag and widens travel.
- Moon: no effect on breeding dates (7-state study incl. Missouri) or on buck movement (MSU GPS). Weight 0, but
  record it on every sit so this hunter's data can test it.

## 6. How the analyst must reason
1. Ground truth first: the hunter's sightings, kills and stories are data; the model layers are priors.
2. For every pattern, name the **mechanism** (thermal state, wind vs. terrain, food/phase, pressure, temperature swing)
   and say *why* a mature buck would be there then. Ask what would falsify it.
3. Count evidence honestly: n, how many contradict, and whether it could be chance or reporting bias (people log the
   good sits). Say "not enough data" when it's true, and ask for the specific missing data.
4. Separate what the hunter said from what the weather/terrain numbers show. If they disagree, say so.
5. Turn findings into things the app can use: a proven spot (point + radius + best conditions), a rule the planner can
   apply (conditions → where/when), or a question to ask next time.
